### Lubbock Water Quality Overview

Lubbock's water comes from two kinds of sources. According to City of Lubbock water quality reports, roughly 60 to 70 percent is groundwater from the Ogallala Aquifer, pumped from the Roberts County and Bailey County well fields. The rest is surface water from Lake Alan Henry and Lake Meredith, delivered through the Canadian River Municipal Water Authority. The mix shifts from year to year, so the water at your tap can change too. City of Lubbock Water Utilities reports average hardness at approximately 205 mg/L, or 12 grains per gallon, which the U.S. Geological Survey rates as very hard.

City reports describe water as it leaves the treatment system. They do not describe what your home's plumbing adds, and they do not cover private wells. A test at your tap shows your real numbers.

Start with a water test. A test shows hardness, dissolved solids and any contaminant of concern, and it decides whether you need a softener, a filter, a purifier or a combination. ### Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a water softener, a filter or both?**
A softener removes hardness minerals, and a filter removes particles, chlorine, taste and odor. They solve different problems, so many Lubbock homes use both. A water test shows which your water needs.

**Is Lubbock tap water safe to drink?**
City of Lubbock water quality reports list test results for regulated substances. For results at your own tap, a home test checks what the city report cannot, such as your plumbing's contribution.

**Does well water in the Lubbock area need different treatment?**
Yes. Private wells are not covered by city reports and can carry iron, sulfur, nitrate or bacteria. Testing comes first, then the right mix of softener, filter and UV.

**Is a free water test really free?**
Ask exactly which tests are free and which carry a fee.
