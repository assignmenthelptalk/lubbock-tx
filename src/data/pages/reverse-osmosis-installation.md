### How Reverse Osmosis Systems Work

Reverse osmosis (RO) pushes water under pressure through a semi-permeable membrane that rejects dissolved solids. Clean water passes through to a storage tank and a dedicated faucet, and the rejected water goes to the drain. A typical under-sink system has four stages: a sediment filter, a carbon filter, the RO membrane and a final polishing filter. RO reduces a wide range of dissolved substances, including salts, many metals and fluoride.

### Under Sink RO Installation

An under-sink RO system treats the water for one tap, usually the kitchen. It installs in the cabinet, adds a small faucet and a storage tank and connects to the drain. It is the most common home RO setup and gives high-quality drinking and cooking water without treating the whole house.

### Whole House RO Systems

A whole house RO system treats all the water entering the home. It needs a larger membrane array, a storage tank and a booster pump, and it sends more water to the drain. It suits homes with a serious dissolved-solids problem. Most homes get better value from a softener for the whole house and RO at the kitchen tap.

## Is reverse osmosis right for your water?

RO is right when you want the cleanest drinking water or need to reduce a specific dissolved contaminant. A water test and a short conversation tell you whether a simpler filter will do.



### Frequently Asked Questions

**Does reverse osmosis remove hard water minerals?**
Yes, RO removes most dissolved minerals from the water it treats, but hard water scales the membrane, so pairing RO with a softener is best.

**How much water does an RO system waste?**
RO sends reject water to the drain. The ratio depends on the system and water pressure. <mark class="data-gap">[NEEDS DATA: typical ratio]</mark>

**How often do I change RO filters?**
Sediment and carbon filters usually change every six to twelve months, and the membrane every two to five years. Follow your system's guidance.

**Does RO make water taste different?**
Yes. RO water tastes cleaner and flatter because minerals are removed.

**Can you install RO in my kitchen?**
Yes, under-sink installation is our most common RO job.
