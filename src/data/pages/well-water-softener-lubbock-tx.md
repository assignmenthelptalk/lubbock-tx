## What Does Well Water Treatment Involve in Lubbock, TX?

Well water treatment covers four systems: a well water softener, an iron filter, a hydrogen sulfide filter and a well water sediment filter.

<div class="svc-grid">

<div class="svc-card">



![Well Water Softener Installation Lubbock TX](../../assets/images/card-well-water-softener-installation-lubbock-tx.webp)



### Well Water Softener Installation

A well water softener installs after the pressure tank so it treats all water entering the house. The unit is sized to your household's water use and the hardness of your well, and it needs clean water to work properly. Iron, sulfur and sediment must be handled first or alongside it, because they foul the resin.

</div>

<div class="svc-card">


![Iron Filter Installation Lubbock TX](/placeholders/card.svg)

### Iron Filter Installation

An iron filter removes dissolved iron that causes orange or rust stains on fixtures and laundry, and a metallic taste. It oxidizes the iron and filters it out before the water reaches the softener. Removing iron protects the softener's resin and extends its life.

</div>

<div class="svc-card">


![Hydrogen Sulfide Filter Lubbock TX](/placeholders/card.svg)

### Hydrogen Sulfide Filter

Hydrogen sulfide gives water a rotten-egg smell. A hydrogen sulfide filter uses an oxidizing media or carbon to remove the gas and the odor. A test confirms that sulfur is the cause before we install one. <mark class="data-gap">[NEEDS DATA: local prevalence]</mark>

### Well Water Sediment Filter

A sediment filter catches sand, silt and rust particles that cloud well water and clog equipment. It installs at the start of the treatment line and protects every stage behind it.

</div>

### Why Well Water in the Lubbock Area Needs Special Treatment

Private wells on the South Plains draw directly from the Ogallala Aquifer. City of Lubbock water quality reports show that the aquifer also supplies roughly 60 to 70 percent of the city's water, but city water is treated and tested before it reaches homes. Well water is not. Hardness, nitrate, fluoride, arsenic and other minerals occur naturally or from farming in parts of the High Plains aquifer, according to U.S. Geological Survey research, so each well differs.

</div>

</div>

### Well Water Testing

A well [water test](/water-quality-testing/) is the first step of every well job. Well-water guidance recommends that private well owners test for bacteria every year, and for nitrate, arsenic and other substances on a schedule that depends on local conditions, because no utility does it for you. We test for hardness, iron, sulfur, pH, sediment and more, and we can arrange lab tests for bacteria and [contaminants](/water-contaminant-removal-lubbock-tx/). Ask which tests are free and which are billed.

> **Not a good fit if...** your well has bacterial contamination. A softener will not fix that, and disinfection or UV treatment must come first.


## What should be treated first on a well?

Treat contamination first, then iron, sulfur and sediment, and soften last. That order protects the softener and gives you the best result for the money.

<div class="faq-list">

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Can a water softener remove iron from well water?</summary>

A softener removes small amounts of dissolved iron, but heavier iron needs an iron filter first or it fouls the resin.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>How do I know if my well water is safe?</summary>

Only a test shows. Guidance recommends testing for bacteria every year, with nitrate and other substances on a schedule that depends on local conditions.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Why does my well water smell like rotten eggs?</summary>

Most wells need both. Filters handle iron, sulfur and sediment, and the softener handles hardness.
